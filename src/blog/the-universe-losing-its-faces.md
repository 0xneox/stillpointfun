# The Universe Losing Its Faces

Extinction is not a statistic, but more intimate. 
The IUCN Red List now includes 172,620 species, of which 48,646 are threatened with extinction. Before human beings walked the earth, a species became extinct approximately every thousand years. Now it is approximately every twenty minutes.

But what does this number actually mean? What is it that disappears when a species vanishes?

## The Saola  ; A Ghost That May Already Be Gone

The saola was discovered only in 1992 in the misty highland forests of Vietnam and Laos. It sits on a 12–15 million year old evolutionary branch, the only surviving descendant on that branch. The last confirmed wild sighting was in 2013. Right now, the existence of live saolas can neither be proven nor disproven.

Think about that. Scientists have barely observed it in the wild. It avoids human contact so successfully that fewer than a dozen photos exist. Its population is unknown. In 2025, for the first time, scientists finally mapped its genome, from skull fragments collected in hunters' houses. They discovered the saola is split into two genetically distinct populations that diverged 5,000–20,000 years ago, something completely unknown before.

The total saola population likely never exceeded 5,000 individuals in the last 10,000 years.

Here is a creature that may be simultaneously alive and extinct, a biological Schrödinger's cat in the Annamite forest. The Chandogya Upanishad uses an image that keeps coming back to me: a teacher asks a student to break open a fig seed. "What do you see?" "Nothing," says the student. "From that invisible essence," says the teacher, "the entire fig tree grows. That is Reality. That is Atman. That art Thou."

The Saola is the fig seed of biodiversity; invisible, possibly gone, yet containing within it 12 million years of evolutionary becoming.

## The Axolotl ; Creature That Refused to Grow Up

The axolotl, a salamander native to the lakes around Mexico City, never undergoes full metamorphosis. It retains its feathery gills, aquatic lifestyle, and larval features for its entire life, a condition called neoteny. Even more astonishing is its regenerative power: it can regrow limbs, spinal cords, and even parts of its heart and brain without scarring.

It is an animal that biologically refuses the fall into adulthood. It stays larval, fluid, open. And it can regenerate the very organ, the brain that in humans is the seat of the ego, the seat of the sense of separation.

Wild axolotls are on the brink of extinction. The IUCN estimates that only about 50 to 1,000 individuals remain.

The Mandukya Upanishad describes Turiya, the fourth state of consciousness, beyond waking, dreaming, and deep sleep as "not that which is conscious of the inner world, nor the outer, nor that which is a mass of consciousness. It is unperceived, unrelated, incomprehensible... It is all peace, all bliss and non-dual."

The axolotl lives permanently in that borderland; neither larva nor adult, neither this nor that a creature that embodies the state between states. And it heals without scar. Perhaps because it never hardened into the illusion of a fixed self.

## The Pistol Shrimp ; A Creature That Creates Suns

The pistol shrimp is no bigger than a matchbox, but when it snaps its claw, it creates a powerful bubble that acts like a bullet. The snap reaches 210 decibels, louder than a gunshot. The temperature inside the bubble reaches 4,400F, almost as hot as the surface of the Sun.

A shrimp. Creating a microsun. With a claw snap.

This creature does not appear on many endangered lists, but its entire habitat, tropical coral reefs, is collapsing. When we lose coral reefs, we lose a civilization of thousands of species, including this impossible little fire-maker.

The Brihadaranyaka Upanishad speaks of Agni fire, as the first form of Brahman's self-manifestation. The pistol shrimp, in a flash, recreates that primordial act. Sarvam Khalvidam Brahma "Everything is Brahman" this does not mean the world is a flat, dull uniformity. It means divinity expresses itself in infinite, astonishing, inexhaustible creativity. A shrimp that births stars in water. This is Brahman being extravagant.

## The Kakapo; The Parrot That Smells Like Honey

The kakapo is a giant, flightless parrot from New Zealand nocturnal, smells like honey, and cannot fly. No other bird combines these features. What mystifies scientists is how such a species evolved to thrive without flight, relying on stealth and scent in a world once free of mammalian predators.

When humans and rats arrived, the kakapo nearly vanished it had no evolutionary fear of predators because predators had never existed in its world. It was a creature born from innocence.

The kakapo population has grown from a low of 51 birds in 1995 to 236 birds as of 2026 a genuine conservation miracle, achieved by moving every known bird to predator-free islands and giving each one a name and a dedicated human caretaker.

Every kakapo has a name. Every one has a human who knows it personally.

This is Seva selfless service in its purest ecological form. The Bhagavad Gita says: "Perform action, O Arjuna, as a dedication to Me, free from desire, free from ownership." The kakapo conservationists have no ownership over this bird. They serve it through the night, track its nesting, weigh its eggs, and celebrate every chick.

## The Pangolin; The Most Trafficked Mammal in History

Pangolins look like tiny armored dragons. They are the world's most trafficked mammal, sought after for their scales for use in traditional medicine. Their populations continue to decline rapidly.

And here is the strange irony: in 2025, Indian scientists discovered what appears to be an entirely new pangolin species the Indo-Burmese pangolin that diverged from the Chinese pangolin approximately 3.4 million years ago. We are discovering new species while simultaneously driving known ones toward oblivion. We are finding new rooms in a house we are burning down.

The pangolin, when threatened, rolls into a perfect sphere a ball of pure self-enclosure. Ironically, this perfect defense against predators is useless against humans, who simply pick it up. Its greatest strength becomes its greatest vulnerability in a new context.

This mirrors what the Vedas call the failure of Ahamkara ego-armor in the face of genuine wisdom. The shell that protects in one world destroys you in another. The only true protection, says Vedanta, is not armor but transparency the realization that there is no separate self to protect.

## The Root of Crisis

Science can map the mechanisms of extinction deforestation, pollution, warming. But Vedanta goes further: it diagnoses the metaphysical root cause behind all of it.

Maya, is the cosmic illusion that makes the world appear as separate and diverse a profound spiritual misconception that obscures the true nature of reality. It causes us to perceive a multiplicity of forms and identities, which distract us from recognizing the oneness of existence.

Avidya creates the illusion of separateness: we see ourselves as distinct from others and from the universe itself. This separation breeds fear, competition, and endless seeking for fulfillment outside ourselves.

The greatest illusion in this world is the illusion of separation, and it is ultimately the cause of the negative human impact we continue to have on our planet. We continue to deplete and destroy the very nature that we are so intrinsically a part of.

The extinction crisis is not primarily a technological failure, a policy failure, or even a moral failure. It is a perceptual failure. Humanity has forgotten what it is.

## What Vedanta Sees

Hinduism, more than any other religion on the planet, emphasizes the unity of all existence. To see unity in diversity is, in the Hindu tradition, true vision. Hinduism has traditionally regarded nature as a manifestation of Brahman, the divine all-pervading existence, which is both immanent and transcendent. Brahman pervades the universe; Brahman is in everything, and everything is in Brahman.

The most radical ecological statement in all of philosophy may be this three-word Upanishadic declaration: Tat tvam asi thou art that. You are not separate from the tiger, the coral reef, the pangolin, the rainforest fungi. They are expressions of the same Consciousness that is reading these words right now.

When we see the divine presence in all beings, we are less likely to treat others with indifference or hostility. We understand that causing harm to others is, in a way, causing harm to ourselves.

## The Mahavakya and Extinction

The Chandogya Upanishad declares four Mahavakyas great sayings that form the spine of all Vedantic understanding:

Prajnanam Brahma : Consciousness is Brahman
Aham Brahmasmi : I am Brahman
Tat Tvam Asi  : Thou art That
Sarvam Khalvidam Brahma   : All this is Brahman

Everything, absolutely everything, is indeed Brahman. 
Including you. Including this moment. Including the very reading of these words.

Now hold this against the extinction list.

The saola, Brahman in the form of a forest ghost.
The axolotl, Brahman in the form of eternal youth and regeneration.
The pistol shrimp, Brahman in the form of a creature birthing microsuns.
The kakapo, Brahman in the form of honey-scented, groundbound, innocent joy.
The pangolin, Brahman in the form of perfect, spiraling self-protection.

Every extinction, then, is not just a biological event. It is Brahman withdrawing one of its own faces. The universe becoming less able to see itself from that particular angle. Permanently.

## The Deepest Paradox

Here is what Vedanta holds in tension with the extinction crisis and it is genuinely uncomfortable:

On one level, Brahman is indestructible. The underlying consciousness that expresses itself as the saola, the axolotl, the forest that consciousness is eternal. Nothing truly dies at the level of Brahman.

But on another level the level of form, of lila (divine play), of rta (cosmic harmony) each form is irreplaceable. 
The Bhagavad Gita teaches that the self is eternal but does not teach that forms do not matter. 
Krishna does not tell Arjuna: "nothing matters because Brahman is eternal." He tells him: act rightly in the world you actually inhabit.

Vivekananda said: "The whole of life is giving; nature will force you to give. So give willingly. Sooner or later you will have to give up. You come into life to accumulate. With clenched fist you want to take. But nature puts a hand on your throat and makes your hands open."

The 6th mass extinction is precisely this: nature forcing open humanity's clenched fist. 
The question is whether we open it willingly in wisdom or have it forced open when it is too late.

Science seeks answers, but mystery gives it purpose. For as long as the saola hides, the axolotl regenerates, and the pistol shrimp births microsuns, humanity will keep looking eyes wide with wonder into the vast, untamed beauty of the living world.

And Vedanta would add: Keep looking. Because what you are looking at is you.
