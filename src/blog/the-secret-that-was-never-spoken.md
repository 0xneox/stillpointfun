# The Secret That Was Never Spoken

I've been thinking about something that happened in a cave at 12,756 feet in the Himalayas. According to the legend, Shiva chose the Amarnath cave to reveal the Amar Katha—the secret of immortality—to Parvati. He created kalagni, a sacred fire, to ensure no other being overheard. The knowledge was believed to disrupt the natural order of creation.

But I think we've been asking the wrong question about what that secret was.

## The Wrong Question

Parvati asked Shiva why she died while he remained eternal. She noticed he wore a necklace of skulls—each skull representing one of her past births. The question seems simple enough: how do I become immortal like you?

But this assumes the secret is about extending life. About avoiding death. About becoming something other than what you are.

What if the secret is something far more radical?

## The Rope and the Snake

Shankaracharya used a metaphor that has haunted me: a rope lying in darkness. In dim light, you see the rope and mistake it for a snake. The snake appears real. You feel fear. You may even run. But the snake never existed. The rope was always just the rope.

When you realize this, the snake doesn't transform into anything. It was never there. The rope was always alone.

This is what Maya is. Not exactly real, nor completely unreal. Something indescribable—defying categorization itself. If the world were absolutely real, it would exist independently of Brahman. But it doesn't—it depends entirely on Brahman for its appearance. If it were absolutely unreal, like a square circle, it could never appear to anyone. But it does appear.

Therefore, it is mithya—dependent reality. It appears, but only because consciousness exists to appear within.

## The Death of the Questioner

Parvati asked, "Why do I die and you don't?"

The answer is not, "Your soul is eternal."

The answer is: your assumption that there is a 'you' separate from consciousness is the entire problem.

What appears to die is a body. What appears to be born is a body. But what you truly are—consciousness itself—was never born. It cannot die. Not because it is "immortal" in time, but because it transcends time entirely.

The immortal cannot die. But more radically: the mortal never truly existed.

## Nothing Has Ever Been Created

Now here's where it gets shattering. Gauḍapāda taught the doctrine of ajāti—non-origination. Nothing is ever originated. The Absolute is ajā, the unborn eternal. The empirical world of appearances is considered unreal, and not absolutely existent.

This is not metaphorical. This is the ultimate collapse of causation itself.

The universe is not created from Brahman. It merely appears within Brahman, like a dream within consciousness. When you dream, no external world is actually created. It only appears. The dreamer's consciousness never really becomes the dream characters. Similarly, Brahman never actually becomes the universe. The universe appears only within the consciousness that watches it.

Shankara said that 'what exists cannot cease to exist, and what exists not cannot come into existence.' "How could being be produced from non-being?" This is not possible.

## The Three Levels of Reality

Reality itself has three fundamentally different levels, and what is "real" at one level is absolutely "unreal" at another.

**Paramarthika (Absolute Reality):** Here only Brahman exists with no reference to material existence or individual souls. This is the only absolute reality—consciousness alone, unchanging, eternal.

**Vyavaharika (Empirical Reality):** This forms the highest level of relative reality—the false notion of material creation, the body, mind and intellect complex and the conception of being an individual soul are portrayed as an actual reality due to the ignorance of perception. At this level, the world appears real, laws apply, karma works, death exists.

**Pratibhasika (Apparent Reality):** This forms the lower level of relative reality based on imagination alone. This is like the snake on the rope—an illusion within an illusion.

The vyavaharika level cancels the pratibhasika level in the way waking cancels a dream. The paramarthika level cancels both. The world is absolutely real from the empirical standpoint. But from the absolute standpoint, it is absolutely unreal. Both are true. Yet they contradict. This is not a contradiction to resolve—it is the revelation that logic itself is transcended.

## The Pigeon's Liberation

Why did the pigeons who overheard the Amar Katha become immortal without effort? They had hatched from an egg dropped by Parvati, shielded by her divine aura. Hearing the immortal truth, the doves gained moksha and became immortal themselves.

The pigeons did nothing. They merely overheard. They had no practice, no merit, no discipline.

This reveals the deepest secret within the secret: the knowledge of immortality is not something that can be achieved through effort. It is a recognition that occurs through grace.

You do your practices. You do your devotion. You trek the Amarnath. But in the end, you realize that all these practices were grace functioning through you. The seeker was never separate from what it sought.

## The Final Dissolution

There is no teacher. There is no student. There is no teaching. There is no path. There is no liberation. There is no bondage.

All these are appearances in consciousness, like characters in a dream talking about enlightenment.

And yet, sitting in that cave at 12,756 feet, in darkness, with an ice lingam melting before you, something happens. The question dissolves. The questioner dissolves. What remains is not "you" realizing immortality.

What remains is immortality realizing that it alone ever existed.

That is the Amar Katha.

Not spoken in words. Not born from Shiva's lips to Parvati's ears. But transmitted from consciousness to consciousness as the ultimate recognition:

"I am That. You are That. All of this is That. And That alone is."

The Infinite pretending to be finite. Eternity playing the game of mortality. Consciousness hiding from itself only to have the joy of finding itself again.

That is what the pigeons heard. That is what pilgrims seek. That is what the ice never knew it was. That is the secret that needs no secret-keeper.

ॐ तत् सत् — Om Tat Sat

"That alone is Truth."

Nothing else ever was. Nothing else ever will be. And even this statement is false from the absolute perspective. Because there is no "ever" and there is no "perspective."

There is only THIS.

Always THIS.

Only THIS.
